01 · THE PROBLEM

Why engineer IL-10?

Interleukin-10 (IL-10) is an anti-inflammatory cytokine that has been investigated for potential therapeutic applications in inflammatory diseases including inflammatory bowel disease.

One challenge in developing protein therapeutics is balancing stability with biological function. Our project explored whether computational protein design could identify IL-10 variants with improved predicted thermostability while maintaining structural features important for receptor interaction.

03 · COMPUTATIONAL DESIGN

Using multiple models to prioritize mutations

Candidate mutations were evaluated using complementary computational approaches. ThermoMPNN was used to explore mutations associated with improved predicted stability, while ESM-based analysis provided an additional sequence-level evaluation of candidate changes.

ThermoMPNN Designed and evaluated candidate mutations based on predicted stability.
ESM Used protein language model predictions to evaluate sequence-level effects.
AlphaFold3 Predicted structures for selected IL-10 variants.
Structural analysis Examined candidate mutations in the context of protein structure and receptor interactions.

04 · STRUCTURAL ANALYSIS

Evaluating the protein in three dimensions

Sequence-level predictions alone do not show how a mutation affects the physical structure of a protein. Selected candidates were therefore evaluated using AlphaFold3 structural predictions.

Structural analysis focused on whether candidate mutations were compatible with the overall IL-10 fold and whether features associated with receptor interaction were preserved.

06 · RESULTS

Prioritizing candidate variants

The computational workflow identified candidate IL-10 variants with improved predicted thermostability. Two variants were selected for further consideration based on their predicted stability improvements and preservation of structural features associated with IL-10 receptor interactions.
